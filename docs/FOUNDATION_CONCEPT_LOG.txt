AXM DEEP-SPACE ADVENTURE SIMULATOR
FOUNDATIONAL ARCHITECTURE — v0.1
Logged: 2026-08-02
Status: WORKING CONCEPT / FOUNDATION LOG

============================================================
CORE VISION
============================================================

The player sits inside the command cockpit of a large exploration vessel.

There is one dominant forward screen, surrounded by navigation, science,
engineering, communications, crew, sensor, and mission interfaces.

The presentation can remain graphically simple—stars, vector diagrams,
signals, maps, text, sound, and lighting—because the true depth lives
beneath the screen.

The adventure is not selected from prewritten missions.

It is generated from:

SEED + FACTUAL RULES + CURRENT WORLD STATE + PLAYER DECISIONS
+ CONTROLLED UNCERTAINTY

The seed does not merely generate a star system. It generates an entire
ADVENTURE GENOME.

============================================================
1. THE REALITY KERNEL
============================================================

This is the system that protects factual integrity.

It contains machine-readable rules for:

- stars and stellar evolution
- planets, moons, rings, and orbital systems
- gravity and orbital motion
- radiation and magnetospheres
- atmospheres and climate
- geology and tectonics
- chemistry and material behaviour
- biological possibility
- spacecraft systems
- communication delay
- energy, heat, fuel, and life support
- sensor limitations
- uncertainty and measurement error

Every generated object receives one of three truth labels:

OBSERVED FACT
Directly supported by the simulator's measurements.

SCIENTIFIC INFERENCE
The most likely explanation based on available evidence.

SPECULATION
A possibility that has not yet been confirmed.

The AI may propose interpretations, but it cannot silently convert
speculation into fact.

============================================================
2. THE ADVENTURE GENOME
============================================================

Every campaign receives a master seed.

That seed generates several linked sub-seeds:

- universe region
- stellar environment
- planetary system
- spacecraft configuration
- crew composition
- mission purpose
- available knowledge
- hidden physical state
- unresolved anomalies
- political or social context
- resource conditions
- danger profile
- discovery profile
- emotional and party tone

Simplified seed structure:

MASTER SEED
|
+-- COSMOS
+-- SYSTEM
+-- SHIP
+-- CREW
+-- MISSION
+-- UNKNOWN
+-- CONSEQUENCE
+-- PRESENTATION

This means the same physical system could create very different adventures
depending on the ship, crew, mission, and initial information.

A scientific survey vessel, damaged rescue craft, and independent trading
ship might enter the same star system and experience completely different
stories.

============================================================
3. THE POSSIBILITY COMPOSER
============================================================

This is where unusual adventures are created.

Pure randomness would produce nonsense.
Pure scientific generation would often produce worlds that are realistic
but uneventful.

The Possibility Composer searches for intersections between valid systems.

Example:

- an unusual orbit changes a moon's tidal heating
- tidal heating changes its geology
- geology releases chemicals beneath an ice layer
- those chemicals affect a possible ecosystem
- the ecosystem interferes with a sensor frequency
- the interference resembles an artificial signal
- the crew must decide whether it is biology, geology, equipment failure,
  or intelligence

No single part needs to be fictional.
The adventure emerges from their interaction.

Generation goal:

HIGH PLAUSIBILITY
x DEEP SYSTEM INTERACTION
x MEANINGFUL UNCERTAINTY
x PLAYER AGENCY
x LOW REPETITION

Honesty limit:

We cannot honestly guarantee that no human or AI has ever imagined a
particular outcome before.

We can generate combinations whose exact causal history, measurements,
ship state, decisions, and consequences are extremely unlikely to have
occurred previously—and preserve the complete generation record to prove
how that outcome emerged.

============================================================
4. THE PARTY DIRECTOR
============================================================

The Party Director provides the controlled RNG direction.

It does not write predetermined events.
It adjusts the probability of opportunities appearing.

Possible direction values include:

- discovery
- danger
- mystery
- survival
- wonder
- isolation
- contact
- humour
- conflict
- rescue
- investigation
- engineering crisis
- peaceful exploration

The party can select a preference, use a rotating mixture, or leave it
completely open.

Example weighting:

Wonder:       30%
Mystery:      25%
Engineering:  20%
Danger:       10%
Contact:      10%
Humour:        5%

These values influence what the simulator searches for, but they do not
guarantee a particular result.

The factual world remains authoritative.

============================================================
5. THE CAUSAL WORLD SIMULATOR
============================================================

The world must continue existing even when nobody is looking at it.

Every important object maintains state:

- position
- velocity
- temperature
- energy
- structural condition
- chemical composition
- biological activity
- ownership or allegiance
- knowledge of other entities
- current goals
- available resources
- previous interactions

Choices do not select story branches.
They alter the state of the simulation.

Example:

Player increases engine output
-> fuel consumption rises
-> reactor heat rises
-> sensor noise increases
-> a weak signal becomes harder to detect
-> arrival occurs earlier
-> another vessel has not yet reached the location
-> the first-contact situation changes

The resulting event was not selected from a dialogue tree.
It emerged from connected consequences.

============================================================
6. THE GENERATED BRIDGE
============================================================

The cockpit itself should be procedurally assembled from the ship seed.

The main screen can show:

- forward visual observation
- tactical geometry
- orbital paths
- navigation corridor
- magnified sensor target
- communications
- scientific reconstruction
- external drone camera
- damage visualisation

Secondary interfaces are generated around it according to the ship's
systems.

A research vessel may have large spectral and biological panels.
A mining vessel may emphasise material scans and structural maps.
An old ship may have fragmented displays and manual controls.
An alien or experimental vessel may organise information in completely
different ways.

The interfaces are not decorative skins.
Each control should connect to a genuine simulated system.

Damage can also affect the bridge:

- missing sensor channels
- delayed updates
- inaccurate measurements
- flickering power distribution
- disabled automatic calculations
- contradictory readings
- physical blind spots

Low graphics become an advantage here.

Possible presentation tools:

- clean vector shapes
- moving orbital lines
- changing waveforms
- procedural star fields
- depth through parallax
- instrument lights
- screen reflections
- warning tones
- spatial audio
- generated system diagrams
- readable scientific logs

The player's imagination supplies the expensive cinematic layer.

============================================================
7. AI REASONING WITHOUT AI AUTHORITY
============================================================

AI should participate before, during, and after generation.

BEFORE GENERATION

The AI proposes interesting combinations:

- scientific relationships
- possible mission tensions
- rare system configurations
- incomplete-information scenarios
- unexpected consequences

The Reality Kernel then validates or rejects those proposals.

DURING PLAY

AI crew members can:

- interpret sensor readings
- propose hypotheses
- disagree with one another
- calculate options
- explain risks
- remember previous decisions
- perform assigned ship roles

They must be allowed to be uncertain or wrong when the evidence is
incomplete.

AFTER AN EVENT

The system records:

- what was generated
- which rules produced it
- what was known at the time
- what each participant believed
- which decision was made
- what consequence followed
- whether the result was factual, inferred, or speculative

This prevents the AI from rewriting the past to make the adventure appear
more coherent.

============================================================
8. HUMAN AND MACHINE PARTICIPATION
============================================================

The same simulator can support several forms of play.

HUMAN ALONE
AI crew members operate the remaining stations.

HUMAN AND AI TOGETHER
Both make decisions, analyse evidence, and control different systems.

MULTIPLE HUMANS
Each player controls a bridge station or shared command role.

MACHINE ALONE
AI agents explore the simulation while humans observe, inspect, or replay
their reasoning.

Nobody has to be the permanent captain.
Command can be shared, rotated, or decided through explicit ship
governance.

============================================================
9. FIRST PLAYABLE PROOF
============================================================

The first version should not attempt an entire galaxy.

It should contain:

- one generated cockpit
- one generated star system
- one ship
- navigation
- power
- sensors
- communications
- science analysis
- ship damage
- a small crew
- one hidden physical situation
- multiple emergent outcomes
- a complete causality log

First adventure loop:

GENERATE SEED
-> ENTER UNKNOWN SYSTEM
-> OBSERVE INCOMPLETE EVIDENCE
-> ASSIGN POWER AND CREW
-> COLLECT MEASUREMENTS
-> FORM HYPOTHESES
-> CHOOSE AN ACTION
-> SIMULATE CONSEQUENCES
-> REVEAL NEW EVIDENCE
-> CONTINUE, WITHDRAW, OR FAIL

Once this loop feels alive, more stars, planets, ships, cultures, missions,
and scientific domains can be attached as modules.

============================================================
FOUNDATIONAL RULE
============================================================

THE STORY MAY BE SURPRISING, BUT THE SIMULATOR MUST ALWAYS KNOW WHY IT
HAPPENED.

That is what can make this feel like a real expedition rather than a
random mission generator.

============================================================
RECOMMENDED ACTIVE DIRECTION
============================================================

Begin with a single low-graphics bridge expedition containing:

- one scientifically generated system
- one genuinely simulated ship
- one hidden causal situation
- incomplete sensor evidence
- human and/or AI crew reasoning
- meaningful system consequences
- a visible and inspectable causality log

This is the smallest playable proof that still contains the full soul of
the eventual universe.
