# Crew Role Education and Qualification v1

## Principle

A crew role begins with a factual perspective, not a personality stereotype.

Navigation initially sees state vectors, uncertainty, relative motion, attitude error, pointing constraints, maneuver reversibility, and collision risk.

Engineering initially sees power margin, battery state, thermal margin, pressure integrity, life-support margin, repair time, and spares.

Science initially sees measurement quality, calibration, replication, controls, contradictions, claim ceilings, and unresolved alternatives.

Communications and robotics initially sees light time, link availability, pointing, data queues, command integrity, remote-system latency, and force/torque limits.

The commander sees cross-system survival, authority, reversibility, crew readiness, mission state, and latency.

The AI collaborator begins as a systems integrator, checking interfaces, provenance, contradictions, root eligibility, uncertainty, fault propagation, and replay integrity.

## Qualification levels

0. Untrained
1. Familiarized
2. Supervised operator
3. Independent operator
4. Mission specialist
5. Instructor or flight-controller depth

Reading a document does not automatically grant a qualification. Advancement requires knowledge checks, part-task simulation, integrated off-nominal simulation, and explicit verification.

## Shared training

All crew receive:

- immutable roots and authority boundaries;
- integrated spacecraft systems;
- emergency and fault response;
- basic medical and physiological exposure training;
- human factors and crew resource management;
- integrated simulation qualification.

The science specialist starts as the first Crew Medical Officer secondary assignment, but this can be reassigned after training.
